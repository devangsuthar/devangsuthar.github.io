## More Than a Weekend Routine ❤️

During the week I write code. On weekends, I do something that means just as much to me: I volunteer with the **Robin Hood Army (RHA)**, serving food and sharing smiles with people who need a meal.

It began as a small step, but it has become one of the most meaningful parts of my life.

## What Is the Robin Hood Army?

The Robin Hood Army is a **volunteer-driven organisation** that works to fight hunger. Its idea is simple and powerful: collect surplus food from restaurants and other sources, and deliver it to people who don't have enough to eat. The organisation started in India in 2014 and works on the principle that it doesn't accept or handle funds; what matters is time, effort and willingness to help.

Anyone can join: students, professionals, homemakers, even families with kids. You don't need special skills. You just need to show up.

## What a Typical Weekend Drive Looks Like

Every drive is a little different, but the flow is usually like this:

1. **Coordination**: volunteers connect with the team and confirm the plan.
2. **Collecting food**: surplus food is gathered from partners.
3. **Packing and organising**: making sure the food is handled and packed neatly and safely.
4. **Distribution**: we visit locations where people need meals and serve them with respect.
5. **Conversations**: a short chat, a greeting or a smile often matters as much as the meal.

## The Moments That Stay With Me

What stays with me isn't the work itself, it's the small moments:

- A child's face lighting up when a packet of food arrives.
- An elderly person blessing us with a quiet "thank you".
- The shared laughter among volunteers who were strangers a few hours earlier.

In those moments, I realise how much we take for granted. A hot meal, a roof and a family are things many people dream about.

## What Volunteering Has Taught Me

- **Gratitude**: I complain less and appreciate more.
- **Empathy**: everyone has a story we don't know.
- **Humility**: serving others keeps ego in check.
- **Small actions matter**: one weekend of effort can feed many people.
- **Community power**: a group of ordinary people can create real change.

It has also made me a better teammate and professional. Working with people from different backgrounds for a shared goal improves how I communicate and collaborate in every part of life.

## Want to Volunteer? Here's How to Start

1. **Find your local chapter**: search for the Robin Hood Army in your city or check their official website and social pages.
2. **Register as a volunteer** and connect with the local team.
3. **Join a drive** and start small; even a single hour counts.
4. **Bring a friend** or colleague; it's more fun and inspires others.
5. **Stay consistent**: regular small efforts create the biggest impact.

If you're a busy professional thinking *"I don't have time"*, I understand, because I thought the same. But giving two or three hours on a weekend gave me far more than it took.

## A Small Request

You don't have to join an NGO to make a difference. You can:

- Avoid wasting food at home and events.
- Share surplus food with people nearby.
- Spread the word about organisations doing good work.
- Simply smile and be kind to someone who needs it.

## Closing Thoughts

As a developer, I love building products that help people. As a volunteer, I get to help people directly, face to face. Both make me feel useful, and both remind me that technology and kindness work best together.

If this post inspires even one person to serve a meal or share a smile, then it has done its job. 🙏

*"No one has ever become poor by giving."*
